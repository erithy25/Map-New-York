# Every deviation from the brief, with its reason

Brief §12 requires the fidelity report to state **every deviation with its reason**. This file is that
list. It is included verbatim as §9 of `docs/FIDELITY_REPORT.md`, and it is authored rather than
generated, because deciding what counts as a deviation is a judgement and not a query.

Each entry names the subsystem, what the brief asked for, what was built instead, why, and what would
close it. Every entry is drawn from the stage report cited beside it, where it is stated at length.
Nothing here is softened: where a number is embarrassing it is given as measured.

The four **structural** deviations come first, because they constrain everything else. The rest are
grouped by subsystem.

### The photorealism gap, stated as its own item

The comparison assessments are the most direct evidence in the project of the distance between what is built
and a photograph, and three findings recur in almost every frame. They are listed here rather than left in
the individual assessments, because together they are the honest answer to "is it photorealistic".

| # | Deviation | Reason | What would close it |
|---|---|---|---|
| B12 | **Building shells now carry real reflectance; the vertical banding does not.** Measured on the shipped glTF materials in a controlled A/B: on the Brooklyn Heights camera the tonal range (p01–p99) went **0.676 → 0.757**, standard deviation **+15 %**, and pixels below 0.10 luminance **0.20 % → 1.40 %**, with 35.9 % of pixels changed. On the Midtown aerial it is stronger — range **0.511 → 0.617**, s.d. **+44 %**, near-black pixels **0.01 % → 1.31 %**. That is the difference between a massing study and a surface that has depth. | The gap this entry originally described was overstated and has been corrected once already: the shells always carried per-class roughness and metallic, and what they lacked was transmission, IOR and specular. Those are now present. **The boundary, stated plainly: this is analytic shading, not measurement.** No reflectance was measured for any New York building. Every constant is authored per facade class and hangs off ADR-004's inferred class assignment, so it inherits that inference's own `MATERIAL_REAL` bit and earns no new claim of realness. | **Per-building variation is invisible to a plain glTF consumer.** It is a shader expression over the exported `_LIT_SEED_HI/LO`, so a viewer that renders the material exactly as the file authors it sees every building of a class at the class average; only an engine that evaluates the expression sees the spread the renders above measure. **The spandrel banding is not delivered and is the remaining half.** The Brooklyn Heights assessment asks for "strong vertical banding"; that needs a dark band at each floor line, which is neither geometry nor a glTF material property. `shellmat.floor_band_uv` records the expression an engine should evaluate, keyed on the metre UV against the exported `_FLOOR_HEIGHT`. Written up as a gap rather than closed, because a glTF viewer cannot express it. |
| B13 | **Non-building structures do not exist, and this entry understated it by staying at ground level.** The Brooklyn Heights Promenade deck, Brooklyn Bridge Park, the East River piers and the Squibb bridge are absent, so a camera on the promenade stands on bare terrain with its own railing and benches below the parapet line. **And so is the entire elevated rail network.** `data/processed/transit/rail_structures.parquet` holds **8,341 structures — 3,914 elevated (492.18 km), 2,689 embankment (314.73 km), 1,666 open cut (166.46 km) and 72 viaduct (12.86 km), 986.23 km in all** — extracted from the planimetric and OSM sources, tiled, and consumed by **one file in the whole repository**: `blender/landmarks/c_high_line.py`. Nothing in `unreal/` reads them. So the J over Broadway in Bushwick and the 7 over Roosevelt Avenue are open sky, and two of the reference subjects are those exact streets. | No stage produces structures that are neither buildings, roads, nor props; the terrain heightmap flattens the ground-level ones into ground and simply has nowhere to put the elevated ones. The data was gathered and then had no consumer, which is the same shape as B6's `roofs.glb` and H6's three runtime files. | The structures stage this entry has always asked for, now with its input measured: 986 km of rail structure and the planimetric deck and pier polygons, all already on disk. An elevated line over a street changes the whole character of a block, so this is nearer the top of the visual-fidelity list than its position here suggests. |
| B14 | **Water is a mirror.** Roughness 0.06 with no wave normal map makes the East River a perfect reflector of sky and towers; the real surface at that distance is dark, broken and largely non-reflective. The mirrored towers below the waterline are the most conspicuously unreal thing in the frame. | The water normal map is not in the repository (see H3). It is bound by material parameter, so no graph editing is needed. | One texture. |

| # | Deviation | Reason | What would close it |

Section A1 says the Unreal project is authored and never compiled. Getting it to the point where it
could be is its own body of work, and it found eighteen defects. Every one of them shared a shape:

## What is *not* on this list

Two things, stated so their absence is not mistaken for an oversight:

* **Nothing was found where a procedural or approximated result is presented as real.** Every inference in
  this build carries a flag in the data (`*_source` columns, the fidelity bitfield) and a sentence in its
  stage report. The one time that failed it was in the *accounting* rather than the world: the fidelity
  report itself reported 0 % inferred facades and 0 % inferred roofs for a period, because its generator
  counted those bits in a table that does not set them. That is recorded in `docs/DEFINITION_OF_DONE.md`
  and is now guarded by a test.
* **No deviation here was discovered by an agent's self-report alone.** Each was either declared by the
  lane that made it or found by the orchestrator opening the artefact. Four defects were reported as
  successes by the stage that produced them and found only by inspection: 32.8 M kit placements pointing
  at assets that did not exist, a 1,003 km² stretch of New Jersey highland modelled as sea, a packed C++
  struct that made the road graph unreadable to the router, and a character whose garments all carried
  the wrong vertex weights.
