## Verdict — this sheet publishes no verdict at all, because the coordinate it was given stands 7.9 m off the thing it names

**The height probe cast 43 rays and 0 landed on built fabric.** `probe.object` is `null`, `probe.z` is `null`, `subject_visible` is `null`, and there is no `subject_visible_fraction` on this record at all. The record says why in one sentence: *"nothing built stands within 6 m of the subject's coordinate; the nearest built thing is `lm_c_times_square.58`, 8 m away at bearing 112 deg, so the coordinate the item records is that far off the fabric it names; no sightline was tested"*. **The measured offset is 7.9 m and the probe's reach is 6 m**, so the whole instrument came back empty over a metre and nine.

**And the geometry is there.** `blender/landmarks/c_times_square.py` builds Father Duffy Square from real building footprints: the booth as a clear-glass plinth from 0.0 to 3.4 m on BIN 1085637 with **27 red laminated-glass steps rising 4.9 m** over it, the Duffy plinth as a 1.1 m granite block on BIN 1090950 and the memorial's flagpole reaching 8.2 m. All four are in the frame — the red wedge at the left of the plaza terrace and the flagpoles standing beside it are exactly those objects. So this is not a missing landmark. It is a **measured landmark that its own sheet cannot see**, because the catalogue's point for it is eight metres away from it.

**That null then propagates into the camera.** With no subject fabric there is no sightline, so the clearance walk had nothing to rank its candidates on, and `scored_on_subject_sightline` is `false` — which means the walk fell back to the eye-level clearance rule that J79 was written to replace. What closed off the recorded viewpoint at 12 m was, in all probability, the subject: the steps stand where the azimuth is blocked. So the walk moved the camera **18.3 m onto a crosswalk**, off the plaza and into the carriageway, and increased the distance to the subject from 59.9 to **64.2 m** in the process. The frame that came out of it is a street: the lower two-fifths of the render is bare asphalt, with a taxi and a queue of cars along the kerb, and the plaza the sheet exists to compare is a thin pale strip along the left edge. **Measured across the pass, all 13 records whose walk could not score on a sightline were moved anyway**, between 7.2 and 52.5 m. This is J107, and it is the mechanism J88 half-described: there, the walk could not see the blocker; here it could not see the subject.

**The other half of this sheet is the advertising, and it is blank.** The reference photograph is, in content, almost entirely lit screens: the Marriott Marquis marquee, a YouTube board, an American Eagle wrap, Broadway posters and a four-storey pink flower. **This frame carries 331 billboard kit pieces, the most of any sheet in the pass**, and every one of them is blank vinyl or unbound LED hardware by the rule the build states about itself — no advertising copy was invented anywhere (DEVIATIONS B5/B15/B15a). The consequence is measurable: chroma **0.0464** against the photograph's **0.1257**, a ratio of **0.369**. Times Square renders as a set of plain window-gridded office slabs.

**And Duffy Square is a pedestrian plaza with traffic driving through it.** 73 vehicles are drawn, 27 were dropped for standing where the planimetric data has no roadway, and **no vehicle was dropped for being on a car-free plaza** — because that rule exists only inside a hard-coded box around Central Park (J95). The most famous pedestrianised space in the city gets none of it.
