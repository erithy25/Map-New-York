## Verdict — two pictures of different things: a harbour skyline on the left, a wall on the right, and 40 Wall Street in neither at any useful size

**The pairing is not a pairing.** The photograph is Lower Manhattan from the water: the Upper Bay across the whole foreground, a Statue Cruises ferry crossing left of centre, Battery Park's tree line along the shore, One World Trade Center's spire dominating the skyline, and a cloudless afternoon sky. Its EXIF GPS is **1,535 m** from the item's recorded viewpoint on Wall Street at Broad Street, past the **250 m** at which it could still be the same view, and the record says so in its own words: a fix this far out is usually correct and simply of somewhere else. That is J60's shape — the photograph is of where its title says, and the choice of photograph is what is wrong. The building the item names is in it, as it happens: the green pyramidal crown right of centre in the skyline is 40 Wall Street's, a few dozen pixels tall from a kilometre and a half. Nothing on this sheet compares the render against it.

**The render does not show the subject either, and the record says so before the picture has to.** The sightline probe casts **13** rays at the subject's mid-height, **132.0 m** above its ground, and **0** land on it; the line is closed at **35.9 m** by `t_-6_0_limestone`, a building shell, and `subject_visible_fraction` is **0.0**. This is the case J79 names, and J79's fix is in the record too: the walk was `scored_on_subject_sightline`, moved **52.2 m** onto the nearest roadbed because the recorded viewpoint had **32 m** of open air against the **80 m** needed, and its `subject_sightline_at_choice` already read **0 of 13**. The walk knew no candidate within reach could see the subject and took the one with the most clearance. The picture agrees with the probe. The middle of the frame, from a quarter of the way in to well past the centre, is the sunlit face of one pale limestone slab rising out of the top of the picture; its corner carries a crumpled streaked band the full height of the frame, and its far flank falls into shade beyond the corner. The Sun at azimuth **242.9°** stands close to directly behind a camera aimed at **70.0°**, and that face carries the cast shadow of a stepped tower with a spire standing behind the camera — soft-edged, with the face's horizontal courses running through it. It is a shadow on stone, not a reflection in glass: the record's only entry for the surface in that column is `t_-6_0_limestone`, and the record lists One Wall Street and Trinity Church among the eight landmark models behind or aside the frame without saying which casts it. At the far right a separate tan-edged, dark-grey crumpled surface fills the last fifth of the frame. The only sky is a wedge at the top left with a dark-windowed tower top leaning into it. The pyramidal crown the viewpoint note says the camera is looking at is not in the frame.

**What the record does establish is the building's presence and height at its coordinate.** The height probe drops **43** rays about the subject's coordinate and **43** land on built fabric, the highest on `lm_40_wall_street.2` at **264.55 m** above a ground at **8.21 m**. The catalogue entry `40_wall_street` stands **14.4 m** away and publishes **282.5 m** — that figure is the tip of the spire and the probe's is the highest surface a vertical ray finds at the coordinate itself, below the spire's point (J74 measures rather than matches). The model is there and it is the right model; from **200 m** its top stands **266 m** above the lens, **53°** above the horizon, which is why the lens was widened to the **18 mm** floor and the axis then tilted **+30.7°** (I18: lens widened before tilt; tilt declared). The record itself says the verticals converge and the frame is not comparable on proportion. A reader must not take the two halves' luminance or chroma as evidence about the city, nor the sheet as evidence of what 40 Wall Street looks like.

## What does not match

* **Subject.** The photograph has 40 Wall Street's crown at the far end of a skyline; the render has a limestone wall closed at **35.9 m** with **0 of 13** rays on the subject. Neither half is a picture of the building.
* **Viewpoint.** Water, a ferry, a shore and a skyline against a **1,535 m** displacement; the render is a canyon looked up at **+30.7°** with a **90.0°** lens.
* **Light, as stops.** The render needed **+6.00** stops to reach middle grey and wanted **+6.17**, held at the clamp; the physical rule alone would have given **+0.95**; linear median **0.002503** against the **0.18** target. The record's own note says a scene this far from a photographable level is not developed into a picture of one, and above +4 it reads under-lit. The photograph's exposure offset is **0.939** stops against the render's **0.104**, a difference of **-0.835**. The metered halves then sit close in mean (**0.5166** against **0.5557**, ratio **0.93**) and at p50 (**0.477** against **0.6222**, **0.767**); that is the development, not the scene — a shaded canyon lifted six stops beside an open harbour.
* **Chroma.** Render **0.0393** against the photograph's **0.1763** (ratio **0.223**). The photograph is blue water, blue sky and a tan, red-brown and green-glass skyline; the render is grey stone, grey glass and a strip of pale sky.
* **The slab's material.** The limestone face carries a crumpled, streaked band the height of its corner, the photographic limestone map reading as damage rather than ashlar where the two faces meet.
* **No people or vehicles in frame.** **406** people and **81** vehicles are placed, but the **+30.7°** tilt takes the roadway out of the picture, and the photograph's foreground is water.
